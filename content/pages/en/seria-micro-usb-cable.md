## What You Actually Get for 110 Yen

The cable I bought is a white **USB-A to MicroUSB** cable. Looking at the front of the package, it covers a surprisingly complete set of features for 110 yen:

1. **Both charging and data transfer (`充電・転送ケーブル`)**
   Some ultra-cheap cables are charge-only, which catches people off guard when they try to connect a device to a PC. This one supports both charging and data transfer.
2. **Fast charging support (`急速充電`)**
   The upper-left corner clearly marks fast-charging support (be sure to check the back of the package for supported output limits and adapter requirements).
3. **A generous 1.5m length**
   Short cables can feel awkward when your outlet or PC USB port is slightly out of reach. At **1.5 meters**, this gives you comfortable slack for a desk or bedside setup.
4. **Reinforced strain relief (`断線防止`)**
   This is the detail I appreciated most. USB cables usually fail right behind the connector plug where bending stress builds up. Both the USB-A and MicroUSB ends have thick, ribbed strain-relief collars designed to help prevent breakage.

The clean white finish and neat packaging also look surprisingly refined—nothing about the presentation feels sloppy despite the 110-yen price tag.

## Quick Tips If You Buy Cables at a 100-Yen Shop in Japan

Note that this is a first-look post based on the newly purchased item and its packaging rather than a long-term stress test. If you decide to pick up a cable at Seria or another 100-yen shop in Japan, a few quick checks will help you grab the right one:

- **Confirm the connector shape:** MicroUSB and USB-C packages often hang side by side, so double-check the front diagram before heading to the register.
- **Check for `充電・転送` vs `充電専用`:** Look for `充電・転送` (charging & transfer) if you want data transfer, rather than `充電専用` (charge-only).
- **Pick the right length:** Shorter cables work well for carrying around with a power bank, while 1.5m is much easier to live with at a desk or wall outlet.
- **Read the back of the package:** Check the rated output limits and device precautions printed on the back before plugging it into higher-power chargers.
